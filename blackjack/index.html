Double Down Madness: you start with just one card, can hit or double repeatedly in any order (doubling doesn't end your turn), no splitting — but if your first card is an ace, you get exactly one more card no matter what. Dealer busting on exactly 22 pushes your bets instead of paying them. Table manners

The strategy matters, but so does not looking like a first-timer. A few real-table habits worth knowing.

  • Use hand signals along with talking — tap the felt for hit, wave your hand for stand. Dealers rely on the gesture, not just the word. (Try it here: with Table show on, tap your hand to hit and swipe across it to stand.)
  • Once your cards are dealt face up, don't touch them. The dealer moves everything; you only touch chips.
  • Chips go in the betting circle in front of you — never handed straight to the dealer.
  • Tipping the dealer (called a "toke") is optional, but common after a win.
  • Keep drinks and phones off the felt — most dealers will ask you to move them.
