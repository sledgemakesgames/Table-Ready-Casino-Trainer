How this table plays

This is Face-Up Pai Gow: the dealer sets and shows his hand before you set yours. Both of your hands must beat both of his to win. Win one and lose one is a push. Exact ties go to the dealer. Fouling (a Low Hand that outranks your High Hand) loses. There is no commission — but if the dealer's best hand is Ace-high with no pair, your Ante automatically pushes.

Hand rankings

Standard poker hand rankings apply, with two Pai Gow-specific exceptions. The joker is semi-wild: it plays as an ace, or completes a straight, flush, or straight flush — whichever makes the best hand.

Five Aces (four aces + the joker) is the single highest hand, above even a Royal Flush.

The wheel (A-2-3-4-5) is a valid straight, but ranks as the second-highest straight — beaten by every other straight, even a low one like 6-5-4-3-2.

Rules cross-checked against Wizard of Odds, Pennsylvania Gaming Code §645a.6, and the official Hard Rock Atlantic City Face-Up Pai Gow Poker guide. The dealer's hand-setting logic here is a simplified house way, not the full casino-specific optimal ruleset.

Table manners

A few real-table habits worth knowing before you sit down at Pai Gow.

  • Set your hand with only your own seven cards, and keep the Low Hand (two cards) separate from the High Hand (five). Which is which matters — in a casino the Low Hand goes in front.
  • Once you've set your hand, don't touch it again. At a real table, the dealer compares the hands and your cards are locked.
  • Don't touch the dealer's cards or the deck.
  • Chips go in the marked betting area — never handed straight to the dealer. The dealer pays and collects.
  • Tipping the dealer (a "toke") is optional but common in North American casinos.
