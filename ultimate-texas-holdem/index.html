Table manners

A few real-table habits worth knowing before you sit down at Ultimate Texas Hold'em.

  • Look only at your own hole cards, and keep them shielded from other players (and from the overhead camera) — this is standard practice at a real table, not just for privacy.
  • You're only playing against the dealer here. Other seats at a real table don't affect your hand at all, unlike home poker — there's no bluffing or folding to "protect" anyone but yourself.
  • Once you check or bet, that decision is locked in for the hand — there's no taking it back, so take your time before you act, not after.
  • Chips go in the marked betting circles, not handed directly to the dealer. The Ante and Blind stay equal by design; the Play bet has its own spot once you commit it.
  • Tipping the dealer (a "toke") is optional but common in North American casinos — place a chip near your bet and let them know it's for them.
